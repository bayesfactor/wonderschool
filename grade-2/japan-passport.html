Station 1

Island Hunt

Japan is not one piece of land. Japan is a chain of islands in the Pacific Ocean.

For many years, Japan said it had 6,852 islands. Then in 2023, mapmakers counted again with better maps. They found 14,125 islands. Japan had lost track of more than seven thousand pieces of itself!

Try this: if you visited one island every single day, it would take you almost 39 years to see them all.

Four islands are much bigger than the rest. Almost everybody lives on these four.

Station 2

The Mountain That Is a Volcano

The tallest mountain in Japan is Mount Fuji. It is 12,389 feet tall. That is more than two miles straight up.

Mount Fuji is not just a mountain. It is a volcano, and it is still awake. It last erupted in the year 1707.

erupted — when a volcano bursts open and pushes out hot rock, ash, and smoke.
Math moment: this year is 2026. Take away 1707. How many years ago did Mount Fuji erupt? Write it here: ______________

A Japanese artist named Hokusai loved Mount Fuji so much that he drew it thirty-six different times — in storms, in snow, behind a giant wave, from far away, from close up.

Whoa: a second grader in Japan learns 160 brand new kanji this year. They already learned 80 last year. By the end of sixth grade they know 1,026 of them.

Station 4

Games, Sports, and Surprises

The most popular sport in Japan is baseball. About 45 out of every 100 people follow it. Japan has 12 professional teams.

The second most popular is sumo. Sumo wrestlers try to push each other out of a circle. There are six big tournaments every year, and each one lasts 15 days.

Third is soccer. Japan also invented four famous martial arts: judo, karate, kendo, and aikido.

Made in Japan. Instant ramen noodles (1958) · the bullet train (1964) · karaoke (1971) · the Walkman · emoji · the QR code · Pokémon · Mario.

Here is the strangest one. The video game company Nintendo started in 1889 — that is 137 years ago. For its first hundred years it did not make video games at all. It made paper playing cards.

Station 5

The Red Gate

All over Japan you will see a big gate made of two tall posts and two beams across the top. It is usually painted bright orange-red. It is called a torii (say it: TOR-ee-ee).

A torii marks the front door of a shrine. When you walk through it, you are stepping out of the everyday world and into a special one. Japan has thousands of them. Some stand in forests. One famous one stands in the sea, and at high tide it looks like it is floating.

shrine — a special place people visit to be quiet, say thank you, or make a wish.

Here is something surprising. If you ask people in Japan "what is your religion?", about 7 out of 10 say they do not have one. But almost everyone still goes to a shrine at New Year to wish for a good year.

Think about it: can a person do something religious without being religious? What do you think? There is no wrong answer here.

Your job

Draw a torii gate. Two tall posts. Two beams across the top — the top one curves up at the ends like a smile. Then put your gate somewhere: in a forest, on a mountain, in the ocean. Color it red-orange.

Station 6 · The Final Challenge

Word Pictures

Japan gave the world a kind of poem called a haiku (say it: HIGH-koo). A haiku is very short. It is only three lines. But it has a rule.

The rule:

Line 1 has 5 syllables

Line 2 has 7 syllables

Line 3 has 5 syllables

syllable — a beat in a word. Clap as you say it. Ja-pan = 2 claps. Vol-ca-no = 3 claps. But-ter-fly = 3 claps.

A haiku usually catches one small moment in nature. Here is one:

Snow on the mountain

a thousand islands asleep

the train slides past, quiet

Count the claps. 5, then 7, then 5.

Your job

Write your own haiku about Japan. Use a box for each syllable so you can count. Islands, a volcano, snow, a fast train, a red gate, an ocean wave — any of these can be your moment.

Bonus: Fold a Paper Cup

This is real origami. When you are done, it actually holds water. Test it.

Start with a square piece of paper.

  1. Fold the square in half to make a triangle. Point it down like an ice cream cone.
  2. Take the bottom right corner. Fold it up and across to touch the middle of the left side. Press hard.
  3. Take the bottom left corner. Fold it up and across to touch the middle of the right side. It should look like an envelope now.
  4. You have two loose flaps at the top. Fold the front flap down toward you.
  5. Flip it over. Fold the other flap down too.
  6. Gently pull the two sides apart. It opens into a cup!

If it leaks, your folds were not tight enough. Try again — that is what engineers do.
