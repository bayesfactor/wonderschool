3 Name secrets

Company names are picture-words too

You already know that Japanese builds big words out of small pictures — 火力 is fire-power, 水力 is water-power. Company names do exactly the same thing. Some of the most famous companies on Earth are named after rice fields.

Guess first, then tap to flip.

✎

On plain paper: write your own name, then draw the picture it would be if it were a Japanese company name. A meadow? A bright star? A small bear? Make it beautiful — this one is going on your company in Station 6.

4 Big and famous

The biggest is not the one you have heard of

Everyone knows Toyota. It makes more cars than any company in the world. So it must be Japan's biggest company for jobs too?

AEON is a shop. Have you ever heard of it?

More people in Japan work for a chain of supermarkets than for the world's largest car company. Being famous and being big are two totally different things, and grown-ups mix them up all the time.

Here is a stranger one. A company called Sumitomo Electric employs 302,972 people — the fourth most in Japan. It comes 490th in the world for money earned. Almost nobody outside Japan has heard of it, and it makes the wires inside your car.

5 Draw the factory

How does a thing get made?

Pick one thing you found in Station 1. Now draw the factory that made it — but draw it as a journey across the page, left to right.

  • On the left: what goes in. Metal? Plastic? Glass? Sand? Draw it arriving on a truck or a ship.
  • In the middle: at least three things that happen to it. Cut? Melted? Bent? Painted? Screwed together? Draw each one as its own little scene.
  • On the right: the finished thing, in a box, going out of the door.
  • Somewhere: one person doing a job, and one robot doing a job. Decide which jobs go to which, and be ready to say why.
✎

Use a big sheet, sideways. Real engineers draw exactly this and call it a process flow. You are doing a real grown-up drawing.

6 Your turn

Invent a company

You are starting a company. Here is everything you have to decide.

  • Its name. Use the picture-word you drew in Station 3, or invent a new one. It should mean something.
  • One thing it makes. Only one. The best companies in the world usually started by making one thing extremely well.
  • Its mark. Design a simple sign for it — your own, not a copy of anybody else's. Test it: can you draw it from memory? Does it still work tiny? Does it work in just one colour?
  • The hard question. What does your company do better than anyone else in the world? Write one sentence. This is the question every real company has to answer, and most of them cannot.
✎

On plain paper: a whole page for your company. Name at the top, mark in the corner, a picture of the thing it makes in the middle, and your one sentence at the bottom.
