Questions

Work through these with the chart open

  1. Fertility. A country needs about 2.1 children per woman to keep its population steady. How many of these twelve are above that line? What does that tell you about the next fifty years — and which country on this chart is in the most trouble?
  2. Two kinds of freedom. Japan scores 96 on political freedom and 70.3 on economic freedom. Find a country that beats Japan on one and loses to it on the other. What does that pairing tell you that either score alone does not?
  3. Gini and poverty are not the same thing. Find a country with a fairly low Gini but a fairly high relative poverty rate. Read both definitions again and explain how that is possible.
  4. The broken chart. Switch to Literacy. Seven of the twelve countries have no number at all. Why would UNESCO simply not publish a figure for Japan, the United States, or Canada? And where do you think the famous "99%" comes from, if not from there?
  5. Years. Open the full table and look at the year attached to each figure. Find the two measures whose years are furthest apart. What would you have to assume to put those two numbers in the same sentence about "Japan today"?
  6. Build your own. Propose an eleventh measure you think matters more than any of these. Write its definition in one sentence, precisely enough that two different people counting would get the same answer. That precision is the hard part, and it is the whole job.
Every figure here is linked to its source. Measures are not all from the same year and not all from the same organisation — the year is shown beside every value, and mismatches are flagged in red. Compiled 20 September 2026 for Wonderschool.
