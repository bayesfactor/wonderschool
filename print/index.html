Black and white. No colour needed anywhere.

Unit 1 · Japan

  1. 1

    Explorer Sheet

    One copy per student. Six kanji to trace, the haiku beat boxes, and six stamp circles to colour in.

    Why it must be paper: you cannot trace over a character on a screen.

  2. 2
    3

    Country Cards

    One deck for the table. Twelve cards to cut out: Japan, the USA, China, India, Germany, the UK, France, Italy, Brazil, South Korea, Canada, Australia.

    Why it must be paper: four players each hold a hidden hand. One screen cannot do that. The rules stay online.

  3. 4

    Japan Desk role cards

    One sheet, cut into four. Illustrator, Quartermaster, Fact-checker, Analyst — each student keeps their card in front of them for the whole activity.

    Why it must be paper: four students need four different sets of instructions visible at the same time.

Unit 2 · Energy

  1. 5

    Energy Sheet

    One copy per student. A hundred squares to colour sixteen-and-eighty-four, four power picture-words to trace, and the power-island checklist.

    Why it must be paper: colouring a hundred squares by hand is the point — the child feels how lopsided 16 out of 100 is. A screen would just show them a chart.

  2. 6

    Power Source Cards

    One deck for the table. Nine cards to cut out: coal, gas, oil, nuclear, solar, wind, hydro, geothermal, wood and waste.

    Why it must be paper: the game needs hidden hands and cards you slam down. The rules and the sortable table stay online.

Do not print these

  • All the reading. The passport stations, the energy stations, both dossiers, both data desks. Read them on screen, where every term explains itself when you hover it and the charts are in colour.
  • The Control Room. It is a live simulation — it recalculates as the student rebuilds. There is nothing to print.
  • Every drawing box. The map of Japan, Mount Fuji, the torii gate, the chain from the lamp, the power island — all done bigger and better on blank paper.
  • Both writing frames. Each dossier has an on-screen brief builder that checks the work as the student types.
  • The Energy Meeting role cards. Deliberately screen-only this time: four students with four devices each read their own role, and the shared budget board is on the screen they gather round.
  • The group wall chart. One large sheet of plain paper, or two taped together.

If the internet is down

There is a full fallback version of everything in the Drive folder (Japan-PRINT-PACK-everything.pdf) with the complete reading, the lesson plans and the answer keys. Print from it only if you have to teach with no screens at all — the energy unit in particular loses its best parts on paper, because the Control Room and the budget board are simulations.
