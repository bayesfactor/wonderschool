2 Picture-words

Fire power, water power, wind power

You already know some kanji: 火 fire, 水 water, 日 sun. Japanese builds bigger words by gluing small ones together. The word for power is 力 — it looks like an arm pushing. Glue it on and watch what happens.

Guess first, then tap to flip.

✎

On plain paper: invent your own power picture-word. What would cat power look like? Draw it, then explain your drawing to somebody.

4 Sixteen out of a hundred

Japan has almost no fuel of its own

Japan has forests, rivers, hot springs and volcanoes. What it does not have is oil, or coal worth digging, or gas. Out of every 100 pieces of energy Japan uses, only a few are made at home.

How many out of 100? Press the button and count.

made in Japan arrives on a ship

Japan's energy self-sufficiency was 16.4% in the year to March 2025. In 2010 it was 20.2%; in 2014, after every nuclear reactor was switched off, it fell to 6.3%. OpenGov Japan, from government statistics.

✎

On the printed Energy Sheet: colour in your own hundred squares. Sixteen in one colour, eighty-four in another. Then look at it from across the room.

5 When the sun goes down

The problem nobody can solve by being clever

Solar panels are wonderful. They are silent, they need no fuel, and Japan has enough of them to cover a city. Drag the slider and watch what they do across one day.

6 Your turn

Build a power island

You have your own island. Nobody is sending you any ships. Design the island so the lights stay on — all day, all night, all year.

Your island must have all four of these:

  • Something that works in the daytime.
  • Something that works at night.
  • Something that works on a still, cloudy day when there is no sun and no wind.
  • A place to keep some energy for later. Draw it. Where does it go?
✎

On a big sheet of plain paper: draw your island from the side, like a cut-through picture. Label everything. Then draw one small house on it and show the wire going from your power to that house.

When you are done, find the hardest question anyone can ask you about your island — and answer it honestly. Real engineers do exactly this.
