Power Source Cards

Nine ways to make electricity, six statistics each. Here is the trick the game teaches: there is no best card. Change which statistic you call for, and a different card wins. That is not the game being unfair. That is energy policy.

How to play

  1. Deal all nine cards out. Two players get four and five; three players get three each.
  2. The dealer calls a category — “Runs the most”, “Cleanest”, and so on.
  3. Everyone puts down their top card. Best number in that category wins all the cards played.
  4. The winner calls the next category. Play until one person holds all nine.
  5. The extra rule: before you call a category, say out loud why it matters. “Cleanest, because the air” is a reason. “Cleanest, because I'll win” is not.

Sort them yourself

Pick a category and watch the order change. Keep an eye on which card sinks when you switch.

Carbon figures are lifetime medians from the IPCC Fifth Assessment Report, Annex III — they count building the machine and fuelling it, not just the smoke. The oil figure is an estimate; that table does not give one. “Runs the most” is roughly the real capacity factor in Japan. “Cost” and “Land” are simplified 1–5 scores for the game, not prices.

What is actually on each card

The argument to have afterwards. Line the nine cards up. Now cross out every one that Japan cannot dig up or grow for itself. How many are left, and could those alone run a country of 123 million people? That question has been the whole of Japanese energy policy for a hundred years.
