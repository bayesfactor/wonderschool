Fourth grade · the card game

Country Cards

Twelve countries, four categories. Call a category, highest number wins. There is no best card — and finding that out is the whole game.

Try it here first

Pick a category and watch the whole league table rearrange itself. The gold row is the winner.

How to play on paper

Print the 12 cards →

  1. Deal all 12 cards face down, evenly between 2–4 players. Nobody looks at anything except their own top card.
  2. The player to the dealer's left goes first. They look at their top card and call out one category — People, Land, Economy or Economic Freedom — then read their number out loud.
  3. Everyone else reads the same category from their own top card.
  4. Highest number wins all the cards played. Put them at the bottom of your pile.
  5. The winner calls the next category. Exact tie? Leave the cards in the middle; whoever wins the next round takes those too.
  6. Play until one person holds every card, or until the timer runs out — then the biggest pile wins.

Playing with a younger brother or sister? Pair the youngest with the oldest and make the younger one call the category every time. The older one may advise but not decide. That forces them to explain why a category is a good bet, which is the actual learning.

Questions to argue about

Which card is the best card? ⌄
There isn't one, and that's the lesson. Australia has the fewest people and the highest economic freedom. India has the most people and nearly the lowest. Keep pushing until someone says "it depends which category you call."
Brazil has 91 million more people than Japan. Whose economy is bigger? ⌄
Japan's — $4.38 trillion against Brazil's $2.64 trillion. So more people does not automatically mean more money.
Canada has 26 times more land than Japan. Who has more people? ⌄
Japan, by 81 million. Land doesn't mean people either.
South Korea is a quarter of Japan's size and beats it on economic freedom. Is South Korea a better country? ⌄
Genuinely open — defend whichever side you take. The useful follow-up is: what would you need to know that isn't on these cards? Life expectancy, safety, healthcare, pollution, happiness, how much a house costs.
Population: national and UN estimates, 2025–26. Land area: total land plus inland water. Economy: IMF World Economic Outlook, April 2026, nominal GDP. Economic freedom: Heritage Foundation 2026 Index, 176 countries ranked.
