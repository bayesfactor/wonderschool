Japan · the whole unit

4

pages, total, for all three lessons

Everything else is read on a screen, and every drawing is done on plain paper you already have. These four sheets are the only things that genuinely cannot be done on a screen.

Download all four pages (PDF)

Black and white. No colour needed anywhere.

  1. 1

    Explorer Sheet — one copy per student

    Six kanji to trace, the haiku syllable boxes, and the six stamp circles to colour in.

    Why it must be paper: you cannot trace over a character on a screen.

  2. 2
    3

    Country Cards — one deck for the table

    Twelve cards to cut out. Japan, the USA, China, India, Germany, the UK, France, Italy, Brazil, South Korea, Canada, Australia.

    Why it must be paper: four players each hold a hidden hand. One screen cannot do that. The rules page stays online.

  3. 4

    Japan Desk role cards — one sheet, cut into four

    Illustrator, Quartermaster, Fact-checker, Analyst. Each student keeps their card in front of them for the whole activity.

    Why it must be paper: four students need four different sets of instructions visible at the same time.

Do not print these

  • The passport stations, the dossier, the lesson plans, the fact sheet. All reading. Read them on screen.
  • Every drawing box. The map of Japan, Mount Fuji in three seasons, the game character, the torii gate — done bigger and better on blank paper.
  • Neko's writing frame. She copies the four headings into a notebook. That is the exercise anyway.
  • The group wall chart. One large sheet of plain paper, or two taped together.
  • The card game rules. Read them off the screen once; nobody refers back.

If the internet is down

There is a full 47-page version of everything in the Drive folder (Japan-PRINT-PACK-everything.pdf) with the complete passport, the complete dossier, the lesson plans and the answer keys. Print from it only if you have to teach with no screens at all.
