COUNTRY CARDS

Where does Japan actually rank? Print, cut out 12 cards, and find out by beating someone.

How to play (2–4 players, 10 minutes)

  1. Deal all 12 cards face down, evenly. Nobody looks at anything except their own top card.
  2. The player to the dealer's left goes first. They look at their top card and call out one category: People, Land, Economy, or Economic Freedom. Then they read their number out loud.
  3. Everyone else reads the same category from their own top card.
  4. Highest number wins all the cards played. Put them at the bottom of your pile.
  5. The winner calls the next category. Exact tie? Leave the cards in the middle; whoever wins the next round takes those too.
  6. Play until one person holds every card, or until the timer runs out — then the biggest pile wins.
The whole point of this game: there is no single "best country" card. India crushes everyone on People and loses badly on Economic Freedom. Canada is the second-biggest country on Earth and has fewer people than Tokyo's metro area. Japan is 4th in Economy but only 62nd in Land and 30th in Economic Freedom. A country's rank changes completely depending on what you decide to measure — which is exactly what makes "which country is best?" a badly-formed question.

What the four numbers mean

PEOPLE — how many people live there.
LAND — total area in square kilometres.
ECONOMY — nominal GDP: the total value of everything the country makes and sells in a year.
ECONOMIC FREEDOM — a 0–100 score for how easy it is to start a business, own property, trade, and keep what you earn. 80+ is "Free"; 70–79.9 is "Mostly Free"; below 50 is "Repressed".
