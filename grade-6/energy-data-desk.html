The Energy Data Desk

Twelve measures. Every one carries its definition — hover any dotted term — the year it refers to, and a link straight to whoever published it. Two of them contradict each other. That is deliberate, and it is Case File 05.

Do not compare across measures. “Japan is 6th for solar and 33rd for something else, so the spread is 27” is a sentence with no meaning in it. Each measure answers its own question. Read them one at a time.

1 · Energy self-sufficiency

Of all the energy Japan uses, how much does it produce itself? Definition

16.4%

Japanese fiscal year 2024 (April 2024 – March 2025)

Nuclear counts as domestic production under the Japanese method, which is why switching the reactors off moves this number so far. The government target is 30–40% by 2040. OpenGov Japan, from Agency for Natural Resources and Energy statistics.

3 · Primary energy supply

All the energy entering the country, not just the electricity. Definition — and note how different this looks from measure 2.

Total 405.4 Mtoe, Japanese fiscal year 2024. Oil dominates here and almost vanishes from the electricity mix, because most of it never becomes electricity — it becomes petrol, diesel, jet fuel, plastics and chemicals. Shares calculated from IEEJ, Economic and Energy Outlook of Japan, January 2026 (who?).

4 · Who actually uses it

Final energy consumption by sector. Electrifying a country means different work in each of these rows.

Calendar year 2022, IEA definitions. “Feedstock” is oil and gas used as raw material — turned into plastic, not burned — which is why that row cannot be electrified at all, only replaced. Transport is nearly all oil, and industry's heat is the hardest part of the whole problem. IEA Bioenergy country report, 2024, from IEA data. 7 · Offshore wind

The plan that has not happened yet.

~421 MW

operating offshore, across six projects, February 2026

For scale: one large nuclear reactor is about 1,000 MW, and Japan's 2040 target is 30,000–45,000 MW. Japan's sea floor drops away steeply, so most of its wind resource needs floating turbines — a technology that exists but is not yet cheap.

Mitsubishi withdrew after construction costs “more than doubled” against its 2021 winning bids of ¥11.99–16.49/kWh. Westwood Global Energy, 2025. Operating capacity: Global Energy Monitor. Note that “10 GW by 2030” and “5.7 GW by 2030” are both real government numbers measuring different things — the first is capacity auctioned, the second capacity running.

9 · The stockpile

How long Japan could run if the ships stopped tomorrow. Definition

254 days

about 470 million barrels · 31 December 2025

Made of three parts: 146 days held by the government, 101 days that oil companies are required by law to keep, and 7 days held jointly with producer countries. The IEA asks its members for 90 days. Japan holds nearly three times the requirement.

CSIS, March 2026 · Nippon.com. Worth asking: what is the plan for day 255?

grams of CO₂ per kWh. Japan's figure is a 15-year low.

Total greenhouse gases

million tonnes CO₂-equivalent. FY2024 was the first year below one billion tonnes, and the lowest on record: −28.7% against FY2013.

Japan Ministry of the Environment, 14 April 2026 · Ember. Note that the FY2013 baseline is the year Japan's emissions peaked, with every reactor off — which makes the fall since then look larger than a different baseline would.

11 · What households pay

Electricity price for a home, in US dollars per kilowatt-hour.

Average of 2023–2025 quarterly figures. GlobalPetrolPrices.com. Treat these as approximate: household tariffs vary by region, by season and by how much a household uses, and this source aggregates rather than measuring. Japan sits in the middle of the rich countries — higher than North America, well below western Europe.

The exercise. Pick any three measures on this page. For each one, open the source link and find the actual number in the actual document. Time yourself. Any figure you cannot find in two minutes goes in a list called “claims I am repeating” — and that list is the most useful thing you will make all week.
