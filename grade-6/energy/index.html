Japan's Energy

Japan is the world's fourth-largest economy and produces about 16% of the energy it uses. Everything else — the wars, the reactors, the hydrogen, the ships — follows from that one number. This lesson is about what a country does when the thing it needs most is the thing it does not have.

The Energy Meeting

You are the Minister. You have to decide, out loud, in front of people who will remember — and then argue the case against yourself.

The question the whole lesson is really asking

In 2010, nuclear power made about a quarter of Japan's electricity and its energy self-sufficiency was 20.2%. By 2014, every reactor was switched off and self-sufficiency had fallen to 6.3% — lower than at almost any point since the war. Japan burned imported gas and coal to fill the gap, paid for it, and emitted for it.

Was switching them off the right decision?

You are not being asked to agree with anybody. You are being asked to give an answer, with numbers attached, and then to make the strongest possible case that you are wrong. If your case-against is weak, you have not understood the question yet.

The rule, as in lesson 1

A number without a source and a year is a rumour. Every figure in this lesson carries both, and every one links out to whoever published it. Two of them contradict each other on purpose. Find them.
