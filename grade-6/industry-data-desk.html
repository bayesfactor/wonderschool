4 · What Japan sells to the world

Top export products, share of ¥110.4 trillion of goods, 2025.

Look at third place. Japan sells nearly as much in machines that make chips as in chips. It lost the product and kept the tools — which is the shape of the whole lesson.

Japan Customs, Ministry of Finance, calendar 2025. Japan imported ¥113.3 trillion against ¥110.4 trillion of exports — a trade deficit of about ¥2.9 trillion.

5 · Who buys it

Destination of Japan's exports, share of value, 2025.

Asia as a whole takes 54.3%; the European Union takes 9.1%. Japan Customs, 2025. Japan's two largest customers are a close ally and a strategic rival, separated by 1.5 percentage points.

Japan's count in the 2026 edition is 40.

Read the 1995 column again. Japan was level with the United States — 149 against 151 — and China had three. Within one working lifetime, Japan and China swapped places almost exactly.

Fortune's own reporting on the decline · Fortune Global 500, 2026. Two cautions. This list ranks by revenue in US dollars, so a weaker yen pushes Japanese firms down it without anything changing inside the companies. And it ranks by revenue, not profit, size or value — a company can fall off it while earning more than ever.

8 · Where Japan still leads

Global market share. Notice what these have in common before you read the note underneath.

Every one of these is a part, a material, a tool or a machine that makes something else. Not one is a finished product a shopper chooses. Japan stopped selling to you and started selling to the people who sell to you.

Chip tools and materials: Brookings and Advantest, 2024. Image sensors: Yole Group, 2024. Robots: IFR, 2025. Zippers: The Conversation — note YKK holds ~40% by value but only ~20% by volume, and the popular "half the world's zippers" is folklore. Pianos: Yamaha's own estimate, FY2025.

11 · Did the help help?

How strongly each instrument of Japanese government support was associated with an industry growing, 1955–1990. Negative means support and growth moved opposite ways. Definition

InstrumentCorrelation with growth
Tax breaks−0.33
Cheap state loans−0.31
Tariffs−0.31
Direct subsidies−0.13

Mining was the largest recipient of cheap loans, subsidies and tax relief, and grew 3.8% a year. Electrical machinery grew 12.2% while ranking eighth or ninth for support.

Do not stop reading here. Governments give money to industries that are in trouble — so support flows towards slow growth by construction, and these numbers would look the same even if every yen worked. That is a selection effect, and it is why economists now argue these correlations cannot settle the question either way.

Beason & Weinstein, Review of Economics and Statistics 78(2), 1996; figures restated in this policy brief. The objection is from Juhász, Lane & Rodrik, Annual Review of Economics, 2024, who note that a government helping losers and a government helping winners are "observationally equivalent" in data like this.

12 · What Japan is spending now

Two figures are quoted constantly and they mean different things. Keep them apart.

CommitmentAmountWhat it actually is
Chips, 2021–23¥3.9 trillion Money allocated. Equal to 0.71% of GDP — a larger share than the USA, Germany or France spent.
Chips and AI, to 2030¥10 trillion A ten-year pledge made in November 2024, covering AI as well as chips. Not the same money, and not yet spent.
Rapidus¥2.35 trillion Government support committed to one company that has not sold a chip. Private investors have put in ¥0.27 trillion.
TSMC in Kumamoto~¥1.2 trillion Subsidy for two fabs — about 40% of the cost. Fab 1 has been producing since December 2024.

RIETI Discussion Paper 25-E-116, December 2025 · the November 2024 pledge. Anyone who says "Japan is spending ¥10 trillion on chips" has merged a pledge with an allocation and dropped the AI half.

The exercise. Pick any three measures. Open the source link and find the actual number in the actual document. Time yourself. Then do the same for one number you have heard an adult say about Japan — and see which list it belongs on.
