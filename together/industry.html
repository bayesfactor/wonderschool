The Cabinet Meeting

You have one trillion yen of other people's money — about seven billion dollars — and one meeting to decide what Japan should build with it. Four people at the table. Every one of you can stop the others.

Nothing to print. Read your role off the screen, or have the teacher read them out. You will need one large sheet of plain paper and a device for the budget board below.

The four roles

Grades 2–3
The Inventor

You are in charge of the only question that really matters: what is it for? Everyone else will talk about money. You talk about what it does.

  • Draw each idea the table suggests. A small picture, in the middle of the paper.
  • For every idea, ask: "who uses it, and what does it do for them?"
  • If nobody can answer in one sentence, draw a big question mark over it.

Your powerNothing is agreed until you have drawn it. If you cannot draw it, the table has not explained it properly — and that is their problem, not yours.

Grades 3–4
The Factory Manager

You know what Japan is actually good at. You have played the Factory Cup and you have the medal table.

  • For every idea, say whether Japan already leads, is behind, or has never tried it.
  • Remember what Japan's wins had in common — parts, tools, materials, machines.
  • Name one country that would be the competition, and say how far ahead they are.

Your powerYou may mark any idea "we would be starting from nothing". The table can still fund it — but the Minister has to say out loud why, and it goes on the chart.

Grades 4–5
The Accountant

You hold the trillion. It does not grow. Every yen you spend on one thing is a yen that cannot be spent on another, and that is the whole job.

  • Run the budget board. Announce the balance every time it changes.
  • For each thing funded, name one thing the table is therefore not doing.
  • Find the cheapest idea on the table and argue for it once, properly, even if you do not like it.

Your powerYou may refuse to go over budget, and nobody can overrule you. You may not, however, refuse to spend — "save it all" is not an allowed plan.

Grades 5–6
The Minister

You announce the decision and put your name on it. Three sentences, and the third one is the one that counts.

  • Sentence 1: what Japan is funding, and the total.
  • Sentence 2: the one number that justifies it, with a year.
  • Sentence 3: the strongest reason this is a mistake.
  • You may only use numbers the Factory Manager confirmed.

Your powerNone. You cannot draw, cost or verify anything. You can only persuade the other three — and be the one who gets blamed in 2040. That is the job, and it is the real one.

The budget board

Eight things Japan could do. One trillion yen. Choose together — and when you are done, the board will tell you which piece of Japanese history your plan most resembles.

Every plan you can build here has been tried by somebody. Spend the trillion and find out whose footsteps you are walking in.

How the forty minutes go

  1. The Inventor draws the empty chart first, for three minutes. A big circle in the middle labelled ¥1 trillion, and space all around it.
  2. Everyone proposes, youngest first. One idea each, one sentence each. The Inventor draws each one as it is named.
  3. The Factory Manager marks the board. Ahead, behind, or starting from nothing.
  4. Spend the trillion. Argue. The Accountant announces the balance each time.
  5. Read what history says — the box under the board. You are allowed one rebuild after reading it, and only one.
  6. The Minister writes the three sentences and reads them to the table.
  7. The challenge round, last five minutes. Below.

The challenge round

The teacher asks; anybody may answer. A correct answer from the youngest student is worth three points, from the oldest, one.

3 pointsName one thing in this room that was probably made in Japan, and say how you would check.
3 pointsOut of a hundred people in Japan, how many work in a factory? (About 15 — the same as work in shops.)
2 pointsWhich company employs more people, Toyota or a supermarket chain called AEON? (AEON, by about 36,000.)
2 pointsJapan lost the memory-chip business. What does it sell instead that every chip factory in the world has to buy? (The machines and the materials.)
2 pointsWhy did Honda rush to build a car in 1963?
1 pointJapan's state fund made about ¥1 trillion across 144 investments. How much of that came from one of them? (More than all of it — over ¥1.25 trillion.)
3 pointsWorth the most: say one thing the table decided that you personally think is wrong, and why. Anyone may answer. The Minister may not.
