The Japan Desk · grades 2–6

Role cards

✂ Cut along the borders · one card each
Grades 2–3
The Illustrator

You own everything anyone can see. You decide the layout — which means you decide how much room everyone else gets.

  • Draw Japan: four big islands in a curve, tiny dots all around.
  • Draw a torii gate and Mount Fuji somewhere on the chart.
  • Write headings in kanji where you know one: 山 mountain, 川 river, 日 sun, 人 person.
  • Write a haiku across the top. Five beats, seven beats, five beats.
  • You draw first, alone, for five minutes. Nobody else touches the paper.

Your powerIf someone wants more space they have to ask you. You can say no.

Grades 3–4
The Quartermaster

You run the Country Cards deck. No number reaches the wall chart except through you.

  • Deal the cards. Find three categories where Japan beats the USA.
  • Find three where Japan loses.
  • Hand each one to the Fact-checker on a slip with the category written on it.
  • Work in a pair with the Fact-checker. Expect arguments.

Your powerThe Analyst cannot write the verdict until you have delivered four approved numbers. They have to wait for you.

Grades 4–5
The Fact-checker

Nothing goes on the chart without a source written next to it. Not the drawings — the claims.

  • For every number, write down where it came from and what year it is from.
  • If nobody can say where a number came from, cross it out. It does not go up.
  • Catch at least one claim somebody assumed instead of checking.

Your powerA veto over everyone, including the oldest person at the table. Use it at least twice or you are not doing the job.

Grades 5–6
The Analyst

You write the verdict at the bottom of the chart. Two sentences, and a third that argues against you.

  • Sentence 1: is Japan a rich country? Commit — no "it depends".
  • Sentence 2: is Japan a free country? Same rule.
  • Sentence 3: the strongest argument that you are wrong.
  • You may only use numbers the Fact-checker approved.

Your powerNone. You have to persuade the Illustrator for space and wait for the Quartermaster's numbers. That is the job.

Challenge round, last five minutes. The teacher asks questions and anyone may answer. A correct answer from the youngest student is worth three points; from the oldest, one point.  ·  Full instructions and timing: wonderschool.vercel.app/together/
