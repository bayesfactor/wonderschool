Second grade · lesson 2

Where does the light come from?

Flip a switch in Japan and a light comes on. Follow the wire backwards and you end up somewhere surprising: not at a river, not at a mountain — at a ship, arriving from the other side of the world.

Start here
Six Energy Stations

Follow the wire, crack the Japanese picture-words for fire, water, wind and sun, taste the energy pie, and find out what happens when the sun goes down.

 One page to print
Energy Sheet

A hundred squares to colour and five power-words to trace. This is the only page in the whole lesson that needs paper.

 With everyone else
The Energy Meeting

You are the Islander. You know what Japan actually has — and it is not very much. Everyone else has to listen to you first.

The one sentence to remember

Japan makes about 16 things out of every 100 it needs. The other 84 arrive on ships. Nearly everything else in this lesson is a consequence of that one fact.

What you will need

A screen for the six stations, one printed Energy Sheet, colored pencils, and several sheets of plain paper for drawing. Nothing else.
